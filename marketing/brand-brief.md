# LIVEOLATOR — Brand & Marketing Brief

> The single source of truth for how we talk about LIVEOLATOR in public.
> Every post, ad, video script, and landing page should trace back to this file.
> Living document — update it as the product changes. Last updated: 2026-07-07.

---

## 1. What LIVEOLATOR is (in one breath)

A **free DJ + VJ performance app** where the music and the visuals run off **one shared beat clock** — so the visuals stay locked to the music automatically, and one person can play both at once.

**Website:** liveolator.zalmanim.com
**Platform:** Windows (macOS planned, not yet — do not market Mac until it ships).
**Price:** Free, everything unlocked, in active development.

---

## 2. The core idea (this is the whole pitch)

Most setups bolt visuals onto a second machine and *hope* they stay in time. LIVEOLATOR runs the DJ engine and the VJ engine off the **same clock**, so:

- Visuals are beat-synced by construction, not by hand.
- Mixing is made near-effortless (one-button tempo sync, auto-mix assist) **on purpose** — so the performer's hands and attention are freed to **play the visuals**.
- One person, one rig, both worlds.

The tagline the site already uses captures it: **"A DJ rig and a VJ rig that share one clock."** and **"Move the visuals."**

**Important positioning guardrail:** LIVEOLATOR is *deliberately not* a maximalist pro-DJ tool. It does **not** try to out-feature Serato / rekordbox / Traktor on DJ depth. That's not a weakness to hide — it's the point. The DJ side is intentionally simple so the visual side can shine.

---

## 3. Who it's for (audience)

Primary:
- **VJs** who want a real DJ engine attached, without a second operator or second laptop.
- **DJs who also want visuals** but don't want to learn a separate, complex VJ program.
- **Solo performers / bedroom producers** doing small gigs, streams, parties — people who can't afford (or don't want) a two-person A/V setup.

Secondary:
- Live-stream performers (Twitch/YouTube) who need synced visuals on a budget.
- Hobbyists and tinkerers drawn to a free, actively-developed, community-shaped tool.

What they have in common: they value **the look of the show as much as the sound**, and they're doing it mostly alone.

---

## 4. Why they'd choose it (differentiators)

1. **Shared beat clock** — visuals locked to music automatically (the headline).
2. **DJ + VJ in one app** — no second machine, no second person.
3. **Effortless sync / auto-mix** — the mix babysits itself so you can perform visually.
4. **Free, fully unlocked, actively developed** — and shaped by its users.
5. **Real-time visual engine** — images, video clips, and live camera/capture composited in beat-synced layers with GPU effects (Resolume-style feel, not old-school MilkDrop plugins).

## 4a. Honest limitations (say these plainly — never oversell)

- Windows only right now.
- Early / in active development — expect rough edges, frequent updates.
- Not a deep pro-DJ tool; if someone needs Serato-level DJ features, we're not that.

Being upfront here is a *feature* with this audience (see tone). It builds trust and invites feedback.

## 6. Voice & tone

- **Maker-to-maker, not corporate.** A builder talking to performers, not a brand talking to a market.
- **Show, don't claim.** This is a visual product — lead with footage, clips, screen recordings. A 6-second synced clip beats a paragraph.
- **Honest and specific.** Name the limitations. This audience rewards it and punishes hype.
- **Community-first.** "Built with the people who use it." Invite feedback constantly.
- **Confident about the one thing.** We can be humble about DJ depth while being bold about the shared-clock idea.

Avoid: buzzwords ("revolutionary," "game-changing"), fake scarcity, over-promising, corporate polish that reads as inauthentic.

## 7. Channel notes

**Instagram / TikTok** — The engine of growth. Short vertical video of visuals reacting to a drop. The product *is* the content. Hook in the first second (a beat + a visual snap). Post consistently.

**YouTube** — Longer proof: full performance clips, "how I built a synced A/V set with a free app," tutorials, feature walkthroughs. Great for search discovery and for the description link to the download.

**Reddit / forums** — High-value but *dangerous if done wrong*. Communities like r/VJing, r/DJs, r/livestreamfail-adjacent, Discord servers **smell marketing instantly**. Rules:
- Show up as a maker sharing a free tool, never as an advertiser.
- Lead with the demo/value, be transparent that you built it, ask for feedback.
- Read each subreddit's self-promotion rules first. Contribute before posting.
- Never astroturf or use multiple accounts.

## 9. What success looks like (goal for this stage)

Because the product is free and always-updating, the goal is **adoption + community**, not revenue:
- Downloads / active users.
- A feedback loop (people telling us what to build).
- Owned audience (email list, Discord/community, social following) we can announce updates to.

Track these; ignore vanity metrics that don't lead to installs or feedback.

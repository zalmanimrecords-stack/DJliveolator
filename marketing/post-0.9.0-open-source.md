## 2. Facebook / social — English

**🎛️ LIVEOLATOR 0.9 — three months, and the biggest jump yet**

Liveolator is our free DJ + VJ app, and it runs the music and the visuals off **one shared beat
clock** — the visuals stay locked to the track with no second laptop and no second person.
Everything unlocked, no pro tier, no subscription.

What landed:

🤖 **An AI agent builds the whole set** — point it at your library and it picks records that mix
in key and tempo, beat-matches them, and **renders preview audio of every transition**, so you
hear the blend before you ever play it. Structure detection — intro, builds, drops, outro — runs
inside the app with nothing extra to install.

🎚️ **A set tempo that travels with the night** — instead of stretching every record to one fixed
number, a set walks the tempo and meets each pair at their midpoint. A 129 BPM record that needed
8.5% to reach 140 now needs about **2%**. The tempo only moves while a record plays on its own,
never across a blend.

🥁 **SYNC that lands on the kick** — the beat grid now comes from the bass band, where the kick
actually lives, instead of the whole drum mix. The classic flam of two synced decks sitting a
third of a beat apart is gone. And if something still feels a hair off, **nudge the grid by ear
in milliseconds** without re-analyzing the track.

🔑 **Key detection that's finally right** — on a real test library, correct keys went from 2 in 10
to **8 in 10**. Half-tempo misreads (125 coming out as 168) read properly now, and every
correction you make by hand survives each scan.

📀 **Exports you can hand to anyone** — warped clips land exactly on the beat, mixes keep their
full stereo image, and mix points land where the floor is moving. Before anything renders, the
export gate checks the mix and tells you what to fix.

📚 **A library that keeps up** — scanning a folder touches only that folder, so a small rescan
takes seconds instead of an hour. **AAC and M4A** play, draw waveforms and render. Bring your
whole collection over from **Rekordbox, Traktor, VirtualDJ, Serato, Mixxx or Engine DJ** — tracks,
cues, grids, keys and playlists.

💻 **And the best part: Liveolator is open source.**

All of it is **GPLv3** on GitHub — the beat engine and its phase lock, the GPU visual compositor,
the library analysis, and the MCP server that lets AI agents dig through your catalogue and build
sets. .NET 8 + Avalonia + OpenGL, over a thousand passing tests.

If you write code, there's real ground here: the **VJ authoring screen**, a **macOS build** (the
codebase was designed cross-platform from day one), and **controller profiles** for hardware we
don't own. If you don't write code, you can still shape it — a bug report from someone who
actually plays out is worth as much as a patch.

**Title:** Liveolator — a GPLv3 DJ + VJ app where the decks and the visuals share one beat clock
(.NET 8 / Avalonia / OpenGL)

Most rigs bolt visuals onto a second machine and hope they stay in time. Liveolator runs the DJ
engine and the VJ engine off the **same beat clock**, so the visuals are beat-synced by
construction. One person, one machine, both at once. It's free, GPLv3, and the whole thing is on
GitHub.

**The architecture, since that's what you'll care about:**

- `Liveolator.Core` is pure C# — no UI, no native, no platform IO. Beat clock, actions, mapping,
  playlist, visual scene model. The entire rule set is unit-testable with no hardware attached.
- Every input source — hardware, UI, automation, AI agent — emits the same serializable
  `PerformanceAction`, and engines are driven only through one dispatcher. No engine calls
  another, which is what keeps the audio↔visual link honest.
- .NET 8 + Avalonia (UI), Silk.NET/OpenGL + SkiaSharp (GLSL effects on textures), BASS (audio),
  RtMidi.Core (MIDI), FFmpeg CLI (video/camera decode).
- There's an MCP server in the repo: an AI agent can query the catalogue, analyze tracks, build a
  harmonic and tempo-matched set, and render a preview of every transition.
- 1,000+ passing tests.

**The DSP work in the last three releases**, which is the part worth reading the diff for:

- The beat-grid phase anchor moved from the broadband onset envelope to the low band. On
  kick-and-hat music the broadband fit lands on the off-beat, which put two synced decks a third
  of a beat apart. Fixing the anchor fixed the flam.
- SYNC gates phase-lock on grid confidence: below the floor it matches tempo only and says so,
  rather than locking confidently to the wrong place.
- A chroma bug in key detection — on a real library, correct keys went 2/10 → 8/10.
- The time-stretcher was placing transients up to 10 ms early, by a tempo-dependent amount, so two
  decks warped differently would flam on the kick. Measured and corrected.
- Set tempo can travel across a set rather than being one fixed number: each pair meets at its
  midpoint, the tempo moves only while a record plays alone, spread over that whole stretch. A
  129 BPM record that needed 8.5% to hit a fixed 140 now needs ~2%.
- Structure detection (intro/builds/drops/outro) runs in-process off a novelty curve — no Python,
  no model download.

**Licence.** GPLv3+, and it stays that way. The BASS native libraries aren't vendored — they're
fetched from un4seen at build time under their own licence, with a GPLv3 §7 additional permission
covering the combination.

**Good places to jump in:**

- **The VJ authoring screen.** The GL compositor, the layer model and the beat-reactive generators
  all work; the UI to drive them is the open canvas. Highest-leverage thing in the repo.
- **macOS.** The code is platform-agnostic by design and there's a CI job enforcing it — it wants
  someone to take it the last mile and ship a build.
- **Controller profiles.** MIDI-learn is built and profiles are just data, so mapping a new
  device doesn't mean touching engine code.
- **Visual add-ons.** Generators and GLSL effects plug in through a documented seam
  (`docs/26`), with a built-in VU meter as the reference implementation.

Liveolator is our free DJ + VJ app, and it runs the music and the visuals off one shared beat clock — so the visuals stay locked to the track without a second laptop and without a second person. The last three releases were the biggest jump yet. Here's what landed.

The DJ view — two decks, FX, STEMS, KEY LOCK, SYNC and live BPM counters on one screen.

🤖 An AI agent builds the whole set for you

Point an AI agent at your library and it builds a complete set: it picks records that mix in key and tempo, beat-matches them, and renders preview audio of every single transition — so you hear the blend before you ever play it. Song structure (intro, builds, drops, outro) is detected inside Liveolator, with nothing extra to install. You name the tempo you want the set built at, and every track gets matched to it.

🎚️ A set tempo that travels with the night

Locking a whole set to one number stretches your records until you can hear it. Now a set can walk the tempo, meeting each pair of tracks at their own midpoint. A 129 BPM record that used to need 8.5% to reach a fixed 140 now needs about 2% — every record plays closer to its own speed. The tempo only ever moves while a record is playing on its own, never across a blend, and the move is spread over the whole stretch, so nothing is ever pulled hard.

🥁 SYNC that lands on the kick

The beat grid is now found from the bass band, where the kick actually lives, instead of the whole drum mix. On kick-and-hat music that used to land on the off-beat and leave two synced decks a third of a beat apart — the classic flam. Gone. And if two records beat-match but the kicks still feel a hair off, you can nudge the grid by ear in milliseconds, right there, without re-analyzing the track.

🔑 Key detection that's actually right

A flaw in the analysis was collapsing most of a library onto the same few keys. On a real test library, correct keys went from 2 in 10 to 8 in 10. Tracks that read half again too fast — a 125 BPM record coming out as 168 — read properly now. And any BPM or key you fix by hand survives every scan from then on.

📀 Exports you can hand to anyone

Warped clips now land exactly on the beat: the time-stretcher was placing transients up to 10 ms early, so two decks stretched by different amounts could flam on the kick — measured and corrected. Mixes keep their full stereo image. Mix points land where the floor is moving, and the bass swap trades between the two records instead of dipping through silence. Before anything renders, the export gate checks the mix and tells you what to fix rather than shipping you a broken file.

📚 A library that keeps up

Scanning a folder touches only that folder, so a small rescan takes seconds instead of an hour. Scans save each track as it's analyzed, so a dropped network drive can't throw away hours of work. AAC and M4A files now play, draw waveforms and render. And you can bring your whole collection over from Rekordbox, Traktor, VirtualDJ, Serato, Mixxx or Engine DJ — tracks, cue points, beat grids, keys and playlists all come across.

The library — BPM, key and beat grids for your whole collection.

💻 Liveolator is open source — come build it with us

Liveolator is free software under the GPLv3, and the entire application is on GitHub — the beat engine and its phase lock, the GPU visual compositor, the library analysis, and the MCP server that lets AI agents dig through your catalogue and build sets. It's .NET 8 with Avalonia, OpenGL for the visuals, and over a thousand passing tests.

If you write code, there's a lot here worth your time: the VJ authoring screen, a macOS build (the codebase was designed cross-platform from day one), and controller profiles for hardware we don't own. And if you don't write code, you can still shape it — a bug report or a feature request from someone who actually plays out is worth as much as a patch.
